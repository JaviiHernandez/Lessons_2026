Teacher Guide — The Midnight Hunt
Lesson 3: Trick or Treat · Halloween Characters & Clothes · Year 3 (Age 8–9)
⏱ 45 min 🗺️ 5 Stations 👥 Teams of 4–5 📚 Vocab + Grammar 🌍 Culture A1/A2
📖 The Story — Spooky Town
"Tonight is 31st October — Halloween night! Listen carefully… In the small village of Spooky Town, there is an old, old legend. They say that when the clock tower strikes midnight, five creepy creatures escape from their secret hiding places and walk through the streets! Tonight… it is YOUR job to find them. Go to each station, scan the QR code, read the clues and discover who is hiding there. If you find all five creatures and tell me what they are wearing… you will earn a magic treat 🍬 and become the Trick or Treat Master! Are you ready? Let's hunt! 🎃"
💡 Read dramatically — pause before "five creepy creatures", whisper "they say…", and build excitement before "Let's hunt!" Encourage pupils to respond "Yes!" together.
⏱ Suggested Timeline (45 min)
0–5 min
Introduction: Read the Spooky Town story aloud. Display the poster. Pre-teach key vocabulary (clothes + characters) with flashcards or mime.
5–10 min
Team setup: Form groups of 4–5. Hand out team boards. Assign roles (Reader, Writer, Scanner, Speaker, Decider). Explain the rules.
10–35 min
The Hunt: Teams rotate through the 5 stations. Scanner scans QR → Reader reads clues aloud → team discusses → Writer types the answer → Speaker reports back. Collect treats on team board.
35–42 min
Debrief: Whole-class review. Ask "What is the witch wearing?" — pupils answer using Present Continuous. Check all 5 creatures together. Hand out character cards.
42–45 min
Awards ceremony: Announce the Trick or Treat Master team 🏆. Hand out medals by role. Award diplomas. Optional: pupils describe their favourite creature to a partner.

🌍 Cultural Note — Trick or Treat
Trick or Treat is a Halloween tradition practised mainly in the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Ireland and Australia. On the evening of 31st October, children dress up in costumes and go from door to door in their neighbourhood, ringing doorbells and shouting "Trick or treat!" Householders typically respond by giving sweets, chocolate or small toys — the "treat". The "trick" refers to a (usually harmless) prank the children might play if no treat is given. The tradition has Celtic origins in the festival of Samhain and was popularised in the 20th century through American culture. Use this activity to help pupils understand both the language and the cultural context of Halloween in English-speaking countries.
🎯 Differentiation Tips
💙 Support
Provide a word bank with the 5 character names on the team board. Allow pupils to use a picture dictionary for clothes vocabulary. Pair with a stronger reader for the clue-reading step.
🔥 Extension
Ask pupils to write their own Present Continuous description of a Halloween character. Can they describe a 6th secret creature for another team to guess?
🌐 EAL Learners
Pre-teach the 5 character names with visual flashcards before the activity. Bilingual support can be offered for the clue-reading step. Focus assessment on comprehension rather than production.
⚡ Early Finishers
Challenge: "Design your own Halloween character! What is it wearing? Write 4 sentences using I'm wearing… / I'm not wearing…" They can draw and label the character too.
